Honest limits

  • Rules, not semantics. ClaimCheck matches words, figures and cue phrases. A paraphrase that shares no vocabulary reads as UNVERIFIABLE-IN-TEXT.
  • No fact-checking against the world. The passage is treated as the only source. A confidently wrong passage produces confidently SUPPORTED atoms.
  • The score is coverage, not truth. It is the share of atoms the rules could place against a span. It is not a probability that a claim is correct.
  • Silence is not disproof. A name or year missing from the passage is UNVERIFIABLE-IN-TEXT, never UNSUPPORTED.
  • Atomization is shallow. Numbers, 4-digit years, capitalized name runs, and a fixed cue list. Pronouns, implicit entities and rewritten units are missed.
  • Hedges are sentence-level. One "may" anywhere in the matched sentence weakens a causal atom for the whole sentence.

How a verdict is chosen

Each atom asks one question: can it be placed against a span in the passage, and does that span agree? No span → UNVERIFIABLE-IN-TEXT. Span disagrees → UNSUPPORTED. Span agrees → SUPPORTED.

AI disclosure

The software, tests, sample passages, narration and video were produced with AI assistance (Claude Code) and reviewed by the entrant. The app itself uses no AI model at runtime: every verdict comes from the deterministic rules in src/rules.js.
